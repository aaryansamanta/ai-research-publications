## 🌟 In one paragraph

Biomedical imaging research mentored by **Dr. Christos E. Constantinou**, Professor Emeritus in Stanford's Surgery (Urology) and Human Biology departments. **Phase 1** set the topic: anonymized ultrasound data of the female pelvic floor under voluntary, reflex, passive and combination states, with a stated goal of building a 3D numerical model. **Phase 2** (the *Imaging Analysis and Research Presentation Development* module) turned data from **23 subjects** into a displacement, velocity and acceleration analysis and a research slide deck.

## 🔬 What the deck shows

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Title** | *Functional Imaging of Pelvic Floor Muscles: Ultrasound Scanning of Asymptomatic Subjects* |
| **Method** | Perineal ultrasound, 5.5 MHz elliptical probe; 23 subjects, age 31.1 ± 13.6 years |
| **Analysis** | Video recordings analyzed offline by a trained physician; velocity from differentiating displacement; acceleration from differentiating velocity |
| **Test states** | Voluntary contraction ("The Knack"), Valsalva (passive), cough (transient) |
| **Findings** | Voluntary contraction compresses the urethra and gives it greater support; no significant caudal displacement during a cough |
| **Take-home** | Perineal ultrasound is a non-invasive way to image dynamic pelvic floor function, and image analysis yields numerical parameters |
