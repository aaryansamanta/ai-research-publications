# Drawing-to-Explain Task — Instructions & Rubric

Based on Quillin & Thomas (2015) drawing-to-learn framework. Used in both the Phase 1 survey and the Phase 2/3 pre/post assessments.

## Instructions to Students

> "Draw and label [a plant cell / the DNA → RNA → protein pathway / a life cycle], including anything you think is important to show what's really happening — not just what a textbook picture usually looks like. There's no wrong way to draw this; we're interested in what you understand, not your art skills. Take about 3–5 minutes."

## Scoring Rubric (0–3 per dimension)

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Structural accuracy** | Major components missing/incorrect | Some components correct | Most components correct | All key components correct and appropriately proportioned |
| **Dynamics/variation** | Depicts process as entirely static/uniform | Minimal indication of movement/variation | Some indication of movement, crowding, or variation | Clear representation of dynamic, variable, or non-uniform aspects |
| **Causal/sequential accuracy** | Sequence or causality misrepresented (e.g., arrows implying transformation where none occurs) | Sequence partly correct | Sequence mostly correct | Sequence and causal relationships fully accurate |
| **Explanation quality** (if paired with a written explanation) | No explanation / restates diagram only | Vague explanation | Reasonably clear explanation | Explanation demonstrates accurate underlying mental model |

**Total score range:** 0–12. Track sub-scores separately since "dynamics/variation" and "causal/sequential accuracy" map directly to the target misconceptions (static-snapshot bias, one-way-causality bias).

## Blind Coding

1. Remove all group/condition labels from drawings before scoring.
2. Two independent coders score a subsample (≥20%) to establish inter-rater reliability (e.g., Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.7 target).
3. Resolve disagreements by discussion or a third coder.
