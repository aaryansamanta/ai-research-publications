# Phase 1 Misconception Survey

**Purpose:** Gather initial data on which biology figures/topics typically confuse students, and correlate specific misconceptions back to diagram features. Administer to high school (and optionally college) biology students who have already covered the relevant unit.

**Format:** Short quiz/questionnaire, ~10–15 minutes, anonymous. Mix of multiple choice, short answer, and one drawing item per topic.

**Target topics** (per proposal): cell structure, flow of genetic information (central dogma), life cycles. Add/remove topics after the literature scan narrows this to 2–3 high-impact areas.

---

## Section A — Cell Structure

1. Draw and label a plant cell from memory. Include everything you remember about size, position, and how many of each organelle there are. *(drawing item — see `drawing_task.md` for scoring rubric)*
2. True/False: Organelles in a real cell stay in fixed positions, similar to a textbook diagram.
3. Short answer: In your own words, what does it mean when a diagram shows "the cytoplasm" as an empty, uniform space?

## Section B — Flow of Genetic Information (Central Dogma)

4. In a typical central dogma diagram, an arrow points from "DNA" to "RNA." What do you think actually happens to the DNA in this step?
   - (a) The DNA turns into RNA and no longer exists as DNA
   - (b) The DNA is used as a template to build a separate RNA molecule; the DNA remains intact
   - (c) The DNA and RNA combine into a new molecule
   - (d) Not sure
5. Short answer: What is the difference between what an arrow *shows* in this diagram and what actually happens in the cell?

## Section C — Life Cycles

6. If a life cycle diagram shows four stages arranged in a circle, do you think an organism spends an equal amount of time in each stage? Why or why not?
7. True/False: A life cycle diagram implies that all offspring survive to complete the cycle.

## Section D — Circulatory System (secondary/optional topic)

8. What color is blood in the veins? Why do you think it's shown differently in diagrams?

## Section E — Meta Questions (all topics)

9. When you look at a biology diagram, what do you assume it does *not* show?
10. On a scale of 1–5, how confident are you in your answers above?

---

## Coding Notes for Researcher

For each response, code:
- **Misconception present** (0/1) per item, using the pathways in the manuscript's Diagram Feature → Misconception map
- **Diagram feature implicated** (static snapshot / single-arrow linear flow / no variation shown / ambiguous arrows / other)
- **Confidence rating** (from Q10) to check for confident-but-wrong patterns

Pilot this survey with a small group (n≈10–15) before full deployment to check wording clarity and timing.
