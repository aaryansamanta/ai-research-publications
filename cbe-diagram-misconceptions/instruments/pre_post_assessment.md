# Phase 2 Pre/Post Assessment — Standard vs. Enriched Diagram Comparison

**Design:** Group A studies with the standard diagram; Group B studies with the redesigned (enriched) diagram. Same learning goals, same time on task, same assessment items for both groups. Assessment should evaluate **conceptual understanding**, not recall.

**Administer:** Once as pre-test (before the lesson, to establish baseline), once as post-test (immediately after), and optionally a delayed post-test (1–2 weeks later) to check retention.

---

## Item Types

Each topic block has:
- 2 **factual** items (did they absorb the basic content?)
- 2 **application/transfer** items (do they hold the target misconception?)
- 1 **drawing-to-explain** item (see `drawing_task.md`)

## Example Block — Cell Structure

**Factual**
1. Name three organelles found in a typical animal cell.
2. What is the function of the mitochondria?

**Application / Misconception-probing**
3. A friend says "mitochondria just float around loosely in empty space inside the cell." Do you agree or disagree? Explain using what you know about the cell's interior.
4. If you looked at a cell under a powerful microscope, would it look "clean and spaced out" like a textbook diagram, or different? Explain.

**Drawing-to-explain**
5. Draw and label a cell, including anything about crowding, size, or relative position you think is realistic.

## Example Block — Central Dogma / Genetic Information Flow

**Factual**
1. What are the two main steps between DNA and a protein?
2. What molecule directly carries the genetic message from the nucleus to the ribosome?

**Application / Misconception-probing**
3. True or false, with explanation: "During transcription, the original DNA molecule is destroyed and becomes RNA."
4. A textbook diagram shows a single solid arrow from DNA to RNA to Protein. What does this arrow represent, and what does it *not* tell you (e.g., about speed, reversibility, or how many times it happens)?

**Drawing-to-explain**
5. Draw the DNA → RNA → protein pathway the way you understand it actually happening (not just copying the textbook arrow).

## Example Block — Circulatory System

**Factual**
1. What is the difference between an artery and a vein?

**Application / Misconception-probing**
2. What color is blood inside your veins right now? Why do textbook diagrams often show veins as blue?

---

## Scoring

- **Misconception frequency:** proportion of students selecting/holding the misconception per item, per group. Use for chi-square comparison (standard vs. enriched).
- **Concept score:** sum correct on factual + application items (0–1 per item, partial credit rubric for short answer). Use for t-test / non-parametric comparison of gain scores (post − pre).
- **Drawing accuracy:** score against the rubric in `drawing_task.md`.
- **Blind grading:** strip group labels before scoring to reduce confirmation bias (per manuscript's bias-mitigation plan).

## Target Effect Sizes (per proposal)

- Reduction in target-misconception proportion: aim for a meaningful drop from baseline (e.g., if ~60% hold a misconception with the standard diagram, target a substantial reduction with the enriched version)
- Overall concept score gain: Cohen's d ≥ 0.4, or ≥20% gain on concept-transfer items
- Statistical tests: chi-square (misconception proportions), t-test or non-parametric equivalent (score comparisons)
